tactical-cz · phase 2 + 3 demo

Phase 2 baseline: V-JEPA2-L + BAS head on a Sparta goal compilation

Frozen V-JEPA2-L (Meta, MIT-licensed) encoder, small linear head trained on 4 EFL matches from SoccerNet Ball Action Spotting 2025 (~8K windows, 12 event classes), evaluated on a 2-minute Sparta all-goals-of-2025 compilation from the official @acspartapraha channel. Out-of-distribution evaluation: Czech league + multi-match splice + different camera operators. Honest baseline showing what transfers, what doesn't, and why.

4 · Ask the pipelineNatural language Q&A (Claude Sonnet 4.6)

The three modalities produce derived parquets — readable, but dense. The agent layer wraps them into a context window for an LLM, which then answers questions in plain language with citations back to the data. Below: five pre-computed questions in Czech and the model's answers. The agent has no access to raw video or audio, only the events / tactical / commentary summaries.

Q1.From the tactical layer alone — field tilt, zone distribution, action centroid — what kind of build-up does Sparta favour on this clip? Cite specific numbers as evidence.

Sparta's Build-up Profile (Tactical Layer Only)

Dominant positional lean: deep attacking territory

The median action centroid sits at 89.4 m on a 120 m pitch — that's 74% of the way up the field, firmly in the opponent's half and close to the final third boundary (~80 m). This is not a team sitting deep and building slowly; the centre of gravity is already advanced.

Zone distribution confirms front-foot approach

Every tracked class has its plurality or majority in the attacking third: - Drive: 50% att — carries are predominantly forward and high up - Pass: 50% att — combination play happening in tight spaces near goal - Cross: 67% att — the highest attacking-third share of any class, suggesting wide delivery from advanced positions rather than early crosses from deep

Implication: direct, advanced build-up with width

The cross zone skew (67% attacking third) combined with high pass and drive centroids near goal suggests Sparta favour getting wide players forward quickly and delivering from the final third rather than building patiently through midfield. The midfield zone is underrepresented across all classes (20–30%), meaning the middle phase of build-up is short or bypassed.

Caveat: Only 4.6 players visible per frame on average, so individual positioning reads are limited.

Q2.Které dva momenty má model nejvyšší top-1 predikce a co tam vlastně vidí? Jsou s nimi konsistentní zóna na hřišti a komentář?

Dva momenty s nejvyšší top-1 pravděpodobností

1. t = 29.0 s — Pass (P = 0.784)

Model s největší jistotou vidí přihrávku. Zároveň tam střílí i Drive (0.732 — to je druhý nejvyšší peak celého klipu, viz níže) a Header (0.388). Zóna: attacking third (50 % pasů obecně tam), 12 viditelných hráčů — přeplněná scéna, konzistentní s kombinační akcí. Komentář z t=27.9s říká „Sadík na Brejky" a je označen jako Drive — model souhlasí s dynamikou akce (Drive 0.73), ale top-1 hodí Pass. Shoda: částečná — komentář i model zachytí pohybovou akci, ale neshodnou se přesně na třídě.

2. t = 20.0 s — Drive (P = 0.732)

Model vidí vedení míče. Žádný komentářový segment přesně na t=20s není, takže konzistenci s komentářem nelze ověřit. Zóna: Drive má 50 % attacking + 27 % defensive share — tento peak pravděpodobně v útočné části. Data to nezpřesňují na konkrétní zónu pro t=20s.

Klíčové omezení: Goal třída má max P=0.029 přes celý klip, přestože komentář hlásí 5 gólů. Model systematicky selhává na Goal — 16 tréninkových příkladů je nedostatečných.

Goal's model max across the entire 116s clip is P=0.029 at t=107s — never at any commentary-confirmed goal moment.

What This Tells Us

Two compounding problems:

  1. Severe class imbalance: The Goal class has only 16 training examples (stated system limitation). The model has essentially not learned what a goal looks like visually.

  2. Visual ambiguity post-shot: The broadcast likely cuts to replays, celebrations, or wide shots immediately after a goal. These frames resemble Out (ball leaves play) or Drive (players moving) more than the sparse goal-scoring frames in training.

Bottom line: Do not trust the Goal classifier here. Commentary is a far more reliable signal for goal detection in this clip than the BAS head.

Q4.Coach se ptá: 'Proč ten model nepozná góly?' Vysvětli to lidsky, bez ML žargonu, v jedné minutě řeči.

Hele, problém je jednoduchý: model nikdy pořádně neviděl, jak gól vypadá.

Když jsme ho trénovali, měl k dispozici jen 16 příkladů gólu z celého tréninku. To je jako kdybyste chtěli naučit brankáře chytat penalty tím, že mu ukážete 16 videí — a pak ho postavíte do zápasu. Nestačí to.

A teď se podívej na konkrétní čísla z tohoto klipu: komentátor křičí „gól!" celkem pětkrát — v 9, 31, 59, 70, 75 a 81 sekundě. Co model vidí pokaždé? Maximálně P(Goal)=0.01. Místo toho mu to připadá jako Drive nebo Out — tedy rozehrávka nebo míč v autu. Proč? Protože gól a situace těsně po gólu vypadají na kameře podobně: hráči se zastavují, kamera se přesouvá, tempo hry klesá. Model to nedokáže rozlišit, protože nemá dost zkušeností s tím, co přesně gól „vypadá".

Zkrátka: model je slepý k gólům ne proto, že je špatný, ale proto, že ho nikdo pořádně nenaučil, jak gól poznat. Potřebujeme víc trénovacích příkladů, ideálně stovky, ne šestnáct.

Q5.I have a $50 budget for one experiment to improve this model on Czech broadcast. What single intervention would give the biggest lift, and how would you measure success?

Single Best Intervention: Collect & annotate Goal-adjacent clips

The Problem (data-first diagnosis)

The model fires P(Goal) max = 0.029 across 116 seconds that contain at least 5 confirmed goals per commentary ("gol!" at t=9.4s, t=30.9s, t=58.9s, t=70.4s, t=75.5s, t=81.8s). Every single goal moment is misclassified — dominated by Drive or Out. The root cause is stated directly: Goal has only 16 training examples. This is your highest-leverage bottleneck.

The $50 Intervention

Mechanical Turk / Label Studio annotation of ~200 Goal clips from SoccerNet-raw + Czech broadcast footage.

  • ~$0.15/clip for binary "is this a goal?" label with 3-second temporal window = ~330 clips annotated
  • Filter to ~200 confirmed goals + 200 hard negatives (shots saved, drives near goal)
  • Fine-tune only the classifier head (freeze V-JEPA2 encoder) — compute cost is negligible

5 · FindingsWhat works, what doesn't, where to look next

Two clear results from running all three layers on this clip. Each survives the smell test independently across modalities.

★ What transfers: temporal Shot localization across leagues

Training was on 4 English Football League matches. Inference is on Czech-league broadcast (Fortuna Liga), with different camera operators, kit colours, stadium lighting, even slow-motion replays. The model still produces a coherent Shot timeline: spikes at the moments the compilation cuts to actual shooting plays, drops to background between. This is pure cross-domain transfer from the frozen V-JEPA2 features; the head only adapts the readout, the encoder never saw Czech video.

P(Shot) along the 120.0-second compilation. Spikes co-locate with the cuts to shooting plays.

✗ What doesn't: the Goal class is structurally dead

On a video literally titled "All Goals of 2025", the model's Goal-class probability never exceeds P=0.03. Two reasons compound:

  1. Long-tail in training data. SoccerNet BAS 2025 train split has just 16 Goal annotations across 4 matches (vs 5206 Passes, 5188 Drives). 16 examples is below the floor for a 12-class linear head to learn a distinct visual signature, so Goal collapses into Shot at inference time.
  2. Cross-modal confirmation. Whisper picked up "gol!" 6 times in this clip — but only 0 of those moments line up with a high Goal-class probability from the events model. The audio modality keeps saying "Goal" while the video modality stays silent. The disagreement is the diagnostic.

Three plausible fixes for Phase 4+: self-supervised pretraining on the 500 unannotated SoccerNet matches, class-conditional augmentation on the 16 Goal windows (temporal jitter, crop, brightness), or LoRA fine-tune the V-JEPA2 backbone on BAS so the encoder learns the "ball-crosses-line" geometry directly. The last one needs a ~$5 RunPod budget.

✗ Calibration biases on common classes

Beyond Goal, two other classes mis-behave: Out and Ball Player Block fire systematically high across all windows (mean ≈ 0.6, calibration ratio ≈ 0.9 vs 0.1 expected). This is the well-known artefact of multi-label BCE without per-class temperature scaling on imbalanced data. Per-class threshold tuning on validation (see appendix) recovers most of the signal at inference time; proper temperature scaling would fix the logits themselves.

Training summary

Frozen V-JEPA2-L backbone (326 M params, MIT), small linear head (1024 → 12 BAS classes). Training: 4 EFL matches × ~2000 windows = 8102 training clips, BCEWithLogits with uniform positive weights, AdamW + cosine schedule, 30 epochs. Final train loss 0.18, val loss 0.21. Per-class val F1 (with tuned thresholds): Drive 0.85, Pass 0.83, Free Kick 0.59, Shot 0.51, Header 0.49, Cross 0.46, Goal 0.0 (too few positives).

6 · Appendix · ReproduceRun this on your own clip

Full pipeline runs on Apple silicon, no GPU rental. With your own broadcast mp4 and a copy of the trained head:

# 1. Vision: per-frame player + pitch detection
uv run python -m tactical_cz.vision \
    --input data/raw/your_clip.mp4 \
    --out data/processed/vision_tracking.parquet

# 2. Events: V-JEPA2 + BAS head sliding-window inference
uv run python -m tactical_cz.events.infer \
    --source data/raw/your_clip.mp4 \
    --checkpoint checkpoints/head_best.pt \
    --out data/processed/events_timeline.parquet

# 3. Audio: Whisper Czech commentary transcript
uv run python -m tactical_cz.audio.transcribe \
    --source data/raw/your_clip.mp4 \
    --out data/processed/transcript.parquet \
    --model-size small

# 4. Render this page locally
uv run python -m tactical_cz.demo.annotate \
    --source data/raw/your_clip.mp4 \
    --events data/processed/events_timeline.parquet
uv run python -m tactical_cz.demo.render \
    --events data/processed/events_timeline.parquet \
    --annotated outputs/events_annotated.mp4 \
    --vision data/processed/vision_tracking.parquet \
    --transcript data/processed/transcript.parquet \
    --source data/raw/your_clip.mp4
